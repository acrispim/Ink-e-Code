7. Safety in outdoor activities

The app is a support tool. It does not replace a printed map, a traditional compass, training, planning or common sense. Never rely only on your phone to find your way.

Compass limits

  • The app's compass uses the phone's sensors. Metal, magnets, cases with magnets, vehicles, electronic devices and power lines can throw off the reading.
  • The sensors may need calibration. Accuracy varies from one device to another.
  • If you use the magnetic declination setting, check the correct value for your region. A wrong value leads to wrong bearings.

GPS and map limits

  • Your position depends on the GPS signal and on the device. Dense forest, valleys, canyons, buildings and bad weather reduce accuracy. If you allow only approximate location, accuracy will be lower.
  • The app uses location only while the compass or map screen is open. It does not use location in the background.
  • Route recording works only while the map screen is open. It stops when you leave the screen, switch apps, turn the screen off or open the camera to photograph a point. After that, your position on the compass and on the map may stop updating. Leave the compass or map screen and open it again before trusting the position shown.
  • The map needs internet to download areas. Without internet, an area may not appear, even if you have seen it before.
  • Distance, azimuth and scout paces between points are estimated calculations. The paces use the value you have configured.
  • A trail that appears on the map may no longer exist, may be closed, may be private or may be dangerous.

Before and during the activity

  • Bring a printed map and a traditional compass, and know how to use them.
  • Start with your phone fully charged and, if you can, bring a portable charger. GPS and the flashlight use up battery.
  • Follow the safety rules of your scout group and the guidance of scout leaders and guardians.
  • Tell someone your route and the time you expect to be back.
  • Children and adolescents must always be supervised by a responsible adult in outdoor activities.
  • Do not keep looking at the screen on dangerous stretches, near water, near cliffs or near roads with traffic.
  • Do not rely only on the Morse code flashlight, the whistle or any other feature of the app to call for help. They may not work on every device and may not be seen or heard from far away.
  • In an emergency, call 193 (Corpo de Bombeiros, the fire department) or 192 (SAMU, the mobile emergency medical service).
